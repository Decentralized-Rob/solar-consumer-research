# Sunrun investor litigation and sales-controls source audit (2015–2026)

Research notes for draft PR #136, prepared November 2, 2026. This record distinguishes separate legal matters, actual court outcomes, issuer disclosures, and follow-up source gaps. The editorial subject is the recurring relationship between Sunrun's customer-acquisition operations, disclosures to investors and financing partners, and the financial/accountability treatment of disputed contracts.

## Additional post-acquisition securities history (2020–2023)

Sunrun closed its acquisition of Vivint Solar on October 8, 2020. The inherited operations and litigation should be identified as **Vivint pre-acquisition matters**, not automatically as Sunrun-originated misconduct.

- **Crumrine and Li v. Vivint Solar**, filed October 2019 and consolidated 2020: securities plaintiffs alleged former Vivint Solar sales practices included forged customer contracts, overstated sales and megawatts installed. The **claims were allegations**, not judicial findings. The litigation continued after Sunrun acquired Vivint. Sunrun's June 2022 **Form 10-Q** reports a **$1.25 million settlement**, final approval May 9, 2022; an estimated $550,000 company accrual after insurance coverage. [Sunrun Q2 2022 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1469367/000146936722000118/run-20220630.htm), and [Stanford Securities Clearinghouse docket chronology](https://securities.stanford.edu/filings-case.html?id=107195). This directly relates to sales integrity versus shareholder disclosures and should be presented as inherited Vivint litigation.
- **2020 Vivint merger disclosure claims**: Sunrun's 2020 Form 10-Q reports multiple suits filed to challenge disclosure around the proposed stock-for-stock acquisition of Vivint. These are transaction-proxy disclosure suits, *not* contract-cancellation or customer-consent cases. [Sunrun 2020 Q3 10-Q litigation footnote](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1469367/000146936720000231/R22.htm). Include in the complete legal inventory but not automatically the main article.
- **2023 short-seller report on customer/subscriber counts**: On October 25, 2023 Muddy Waters published a report alleging Sunrun's non-GAAP subscriber counts exceeded its reported EIA data by ~20.9% and raised a hypothesis about claims for investment tax credits. Sunrun published a same-day response explaining installed subscribers, EIA operating-system and billing data, and ITC accounting as different populations. The comparison was disputed; neither the short-seller calculation nor Sunrun's response is a court determination. [Muddy Waters report](https://muddywatersresearch.com/research/run/just-making-it-up/), [Sunrun's direct rebuttal](https://investors.sunrun.com/news-events/press-releases/detail/296/deceptive-short-seller-muddy-waters-once-again-has-its).
- **2023 PDF misclassified easily as a filed complaint:** [Howard G. Smith law-office sample PDF](https://www.howardsmithlaw.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/Sunrun-Complaint.pdf) is headed "Class Action Complaint" and quotes Muddy Waters, but the first page contains a **blank case number and unnamed plaintiff**. It is a model pleading, not evidence of a docketed lawsuit. A court case number and actual filed complaint remain unverified. Do not report the draft's assertion that "14,390 systems didn't exist" as a proven accounting fact or a filed claim.

## Slovin settlement: concrete oversight and agency-language comparison

The filed *Slovin* settlement, §12.1–12.9, obligates Sunrun/CEE to oversee telemarketing operations, maintain Do Not Call lists, perform vendor due diligence, establish vendor pay-withholding and audit provisions, investigate complaints, retain case records, and conduct quarterly random vendor audits with independent assistance for four years. Section 12.10 specifically provides that compliance with these requirements shall not be used as evidence of control, agency or legal responsibility for telemarketing vendors' alleged TCPA violations. The restrictions and caveat are in **the same document**.

This is evidence of the company's negotiated legal/operational approach, not evidence that any particular post-settlement vendor breached the requirements. Investor reporting should examine what company-wide performance measurements were generated and whether the resulting data appeared in board or financial reporting.

## Important distinctions and research gaps

- **IPO suit versus cancellation securities suit:** The $32M and $2.5M cases must not be merged or portrayed as a single adjudicated claim. The February 2018 IPO amendment added customer-cancellation allegations, but the IPO case also alleged other disclosure issues.
- **Derivative governance settlement:** SEC reports establish the promise and final approval. They do not enumerate the reforms. Need settlement stipulation and order from N.D. Cal. case 3:17-cv-03743 (related Olsen case 3:19-cv-02109). Do not invent specific reforms or claim the policies now disclosed stemmed from the settlement.
- **SEC inquiry vs FBI record:** Contemporary 2017 reporting discussed an SEC investigation. A separately released FBI record says the US Attorney's Office declined to proceed in 2018 due to resources. Those are different authorities and outcomes. Do not interpret the FBI record as a disposition of the SEC investigation.
- **Telemarketing settlement vs later solar-contract enforcement:** Same general sales oversight question, different underlying statutes and practices. Draw a governance comparison without implying identical violations.
- **Financing pool:** The financial representations show how one facility treats eligible solar assets, not a count of customer-contract violations across Sunrun.
- **Q3 reporting:** Compare issuer's legal-contingency discussion, cash generation, subscriber metrics, customer acquisition and asset valuation; seek quantification of complaints, reversals, deficient asset classifications, consent checks and resolved restitution.
