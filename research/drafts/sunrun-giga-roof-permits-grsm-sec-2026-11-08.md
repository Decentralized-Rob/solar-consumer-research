# Sunrun, Giga, and the roof work outside the solar permit

*Research story draft, November 8, 2026. Public records only. This is a source-backed lead for reporting, not a finding about any homeowner's contract.*

A roof replacement and a solar installation can appear under different contractors and different permit numbers at the same address. That paper split matters when a homeowner later asks who arranged the roof work, who authorized it, and who should have caught a problem.

Two original municipal reports show that combination involving Sunrun and Giga Property Solutions or its principal, Luiz Espigar, in different states. A Connecticut lawsuit shows how Sunrun, represented by Gordon Rees Scully Mansukhani (GRSM), described a separate roofer referred by a salesperson. Sunrun's SEC reports describe its customer contracts and oversight at a company level. The records raise a question about where a salesperson-arranged roof falls in that system. They do not, by themselves, prove that Sunrun contracted with either roofer.

These are **two properties**, not a count of two roof projects per solar permit. In Trumbull, the PDF's extracted text can duplicate the roof-permit line across adjoining rows. The permit number RB-25-780 is counted once. The published municipal reports are permit summaries; the underlying applications, authorizations, contracts, inspections, and payment records are still needed.

The Lowell dates also cut against an easy assumption about sequence: its solar permit preceded the Giga roof permit by more than two months. Permit issue dates alone cannot establish when a sale, roof decision, or installation occurred.

## Sunrun's words in the Connecticut court record

*In Mishler v. Sunrun*, docket **HHB-CV25-6096501-S**, counsel Cullen Guilmartin described a roof referral by the Sunrun salesperson this way in a motion filed April 10, 2026: “the sales representative who sold the Agreement and corresponding solar panel system (the ‘System’) provided a personal referral to Plaintiff when suggesting he use New Creation for roof work prior to the System’s installation.” The next sentence says New Creation did the work “to the exclusion of Sunrun.” Those are Sunrun's factual assertions in a filed argument, not judicial findings. [Original memorandum, ECF 124, p. 2](https://civilinquiry.jud.ct.gov/DocumentInquiry/DocumentInquiry.aspx?DocumentNo=32417267).

In the same filing, Sunrun says any company involvement was limited to scheduling so the solar installation could proceed once the new roof was ready. Its June 5, 2026 expert disclosure says a witness was expected to testify that Sunrun had “no right, obligation, or ability to coordinate, oversee, monitor, or supervise New Creation’s work” and that the salesperson's suggestion was “expressly forbidden.” This was a disclosure of proposed testimony; the record needs the actual policy in force when the referral occurred. [Case docket](https://civilinquiry.jud.ct.gov/CaseDetail/PublicCaseDetail.aspx?DocketNo=HHBCV256096501S). The complete disclosure is also in the research owner's *Mishler GRSM Citable Source Packet*, pp. 56–58; an individual public PDF link is still needed.

The October 17, 2025 cross-complaint requested “Reimbursement of any and all monies paid to New Creation for and in relation to the roofing work related to Plaintiff’s claims.” [Original cross-complaint, ECF 106, p. 3](https://civilinquiry.jud.ct.gov/DocumentInquiry/DocumentInquiry.aspx?DocumentNo=31145839). That requested remedy does not establish that Sunrun paid or contracted with New Creation; the payment trail is precisely what needs to be checked against the later referral account. The lawsuit was settled and withdrawn without a public merits decision. [Docket](https://civilinquiry.jud.ct.gov/CaseDetail/PublicCaseDetail.aspx?DocketNo=HHBCV256096501S); [existing source-based Mishler article](https://solarcomplaint.com/research/sunrun-mishler-roofing-lawsuit).

A separate private Massachusetts complaint contains potentially comparable wording from the same counsel and a signed roofer response discussing its contract-signing practice. That confidential source set should be reviewed alongside the public court record before any excerpt appears in a published article. The municipal examples below provide independent places to test whether the roofer's described practice recurs; they do not prove it did.

The company-wide filings and the particular court defense address different defined relationships. They become a substantive comparison if actual contracts, compensation, vendor records, policies, and internal project notes show how roof work was arranged across years. The September 2026 filing reports litigation in aggregate; absence of a case's name does not alone prove a disclosure problem.

## Documents that would answer the question

For the two town matches, obtain each complete roof and solar permit packet, including the applicant's signature or authorization, contractor license, plan revisions, inspection history, and any attached contract. Compare them with the sales-representative identity, roof quote, payment source, and Sunrun project notes for the same address. A permit match supplies a place and time to test the account; it cannot substitute for those documents.

For the GRSM comparison, preserve the complete filed memorandum and expert disclosure in *Mishler* alongside the contract and referral evidence on which they rely. Add cases in other states to a repeated-defense count only after locating the actual pleadings and checking their facts and outcomes. The current public-source set supports one reviewed Connecticut court defense and two municipal Giga/Espigar–Sunrun property matches across Massachusetts and Connecticut; it does not support a quantified nationwide rate of disputed signatures or unauthorized roofing contracts.
