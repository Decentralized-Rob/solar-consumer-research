# Sunrun, Giga, and the roof work outside the solar permit

*Research story draft, November 8, 2026. Public records only. This is a source-backed lead for reporting, not a finding about any homeowner's contract.*

A roof replacement and a solar installation can appear under different contractors and different permit numbers at the same address. That paper split matters when a homeowner later asks who arranged the roof work, who authorized it, and who should have caught a problem.

Two original municipal reports show that combination involving Sunrun and Giga Property Solutions or its principal, Luiz Espigar, in different states. A Connecticut lawsuit shows how Sunrun, represented by Gordon Rees Scully Mansukhani (GRSM), described a separate roofer referred by a salesperson. Sunrun's SEC reports describe its customer contracts and oversight at a company level. The records raise a question about where a salesperson-arranged roof falls in that system. They do not, by themselves, prove that Sunrun contracted with either roofer.

These are **two properties**, not a count of two roof projects per solar permit. In Trumbull, the PDF's extracted text can duplicate the roof-permit line across adjoining rows. The permit number RB-25-780 is counted once. The published municipal reports are permit summaries; the underlying applications, authorizations, contracts, inspections, and payment records are still needed.

The Lowell dates also cut against an easy assumption about sequence: its solar permit preceded the Giga roof permit by more than two months. Permit issue dates alone cannot establish when a sale, roof decision, or installation occurred.

## The separate-roofer defense in a Connecticut case

In *Mishler v. Sunrun*, Connecticut Superior Court docket **HHB-CV25-6096501-S**, Sunrun's GRSM lawyer Cullen Guilmartin described a Sunrun salesperson's recommendation of New Creation, a roofing company, as a personal referral. In Sunrun's April 10, 2026 memorandum, counsel argued the roofer performed work without Sunrun's involvement. A June 5, 2026 expert disclosure said Sunrun had no contract with that roofer or right to oversee the roof work and described the salesperson as an independent contractor whose recommendation was against policy. These are **Sunrun's litigation positions**, not established facts about the underlying transaction. [Court docket](https://civilinquiry.jud.ct.gov/CaseDetail/PublicCaseDetail.aspx?DocketNo=HHBCV256096501S); [April 10 memorandum, document 32417267](https://civilinquiry.jud.ct.gov/DocumentInquiry/DocumentInquiry.aspx?DocumentNo=32417267); [existing source-based Mishler analysis](https://solarcomplaint.com/research/ct-mishler-sunrun-roof-referral).

A different Sunrun filing in the same case requested reimbursement from New Creation of money paid for roofing work if Sunrun were held liable. That contingent request is worth reading beside the no-relationship defense; it does not establish who originally paid the roofer or who was legally responsible for the work. [October 17, 2025 cross complaint, document 31145839](https://civilinquiry.jud.ct.gov/DocumentInquiry/DocumentInquiry.aspx?DocumentNo=31145839).

The case ended by withdrawal after reported settlement, without a public merits ruling establishing the roofer's or Sunrun's version. [Court docket](https://civilinquiry.jud.ct.gov/CaseDetail/PublicCaseDetail.aspx?DocketNo=HHBCV256096501S). The next meaningful comparison is the signed roof agreement, payment trail, salesperson classification, and internal policy in each job. Similar lawyer wording alone would show a defense pattern; matching underlying transaction records would show whether the separation actually holds.

## What Sunrun tells investors

Sunrun's 2025 Form 10-K distinguishes direct-to-consumer sales from independent energy system partners, including sales and installation partners. It says that customer sales contracts in its sales-partner model are between Sunrun and the customer, at a price Sunrun sets. The filing's defined partner categories do **not** automatically include a roofer hired by a salesperson for a separate contract. [2025 Form 10-K, Multi-Channel Capabilities](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1469367/000162828026012289/run-20251231.htm).

In its September 2026 Form 10-Q, Sunrun describes itself as the primary customer contracting entity bearing risks from construction, cost overruns, cancellations, and regulatory compliance. It also says it is ultimately responsible as contracting party for **energy system installations**. The same filing describes standards, written agreements, and oversight of third-party suppliers and energy system partners, while acknowledging it cannot guarantee third-party compliance. Those statements have a defined scope. They do not, without more, make a separately arranged reroof part of a Sunrun energy system installation. [Q3 2026 Form 10-Q, risk factors](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1469367/000162828026070158/run-20260930.htm).

Compare the language over reporting periods rather than treating the newest filing as a one-time pledge: [2022 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1469367/000146936723000030/run-20221231.htm) · [2023 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1469367/000146936724000024/run-20231231.htm) · [2024 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1469367/000146936725000039/run-20241231.htm) · [2025 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1469367/000162828026012289/run-20251231.htm) · [Q3 2026 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1469367/000162828026070158/run-20260930.htm). Its risk-factor wording changes from “solar partners” to “energy system partners” across the period, but retains the selection, supervision, oversight, and legal-compliance theme. The Q3 2026 litigation footnote reports proceedings and reserves in aggregate; a case's absence by name cannot establish a disclosure violation.

## Documents that would answer the question

For the two town matches, obtain each complete roof and solar permit packet, including the applicant's signature or authorization, contractor license, plan revisions, inspection history, and any attached contract. Compare them with the sales-representative identity, roof quote, payment source, and Sunrun project notes for the same address. A permit match supplies a place and time to test the account; it cannot substitute for those documents.

For the GRSM comparison, preserve the complete filed memorandum and expert disclosure in *Mishler* alongside the contract and referral evidence on which they rely. Add cases in other states to a repeated-defense count only after locating the actual pleadings and checking their facts and outcomes. The current public-source set supports one reviewed Connecticut court defense and two municipal Giga/Espigar–Sunrun property matches across Massachusetts and Connecticut; it does not support a quantified nationwide rate of disputed signatures or unauthorized roofing contracts.
